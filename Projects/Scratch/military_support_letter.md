# Letter of Support for Michael Aaron

To those overseeing Michael Aaron's enlistment,

I am writing this letter in support of Michael Aaron's enlistment in the United States Army. I served as Michael's instructor in Econ 411, Computational Economics, several years ago. Michael describes himself as someone who learns quickly, works consistently, and takes responsibility seriously. I can confirm from direct experience that this self-assessment is accurate, and I would like to provide specific evidence from his academic work to support it.

Michael's term paper on India's ethanol market was among the more ambitious projects undertaken by students in that course. The paper required him to analyze a complex, multi-layered problem spanning government policy, agricultural economics, commodity markets, and international trade from data collection through empirical modeling. He gathered data from USDA Foreign Agricultural Service reports spanning over a decade, constructed an original dataset, and built an empirical model linking sugar production to ethanol output. Michael used Python (pandas, NumPy, statsmodels, matplotlib, and seaborn) to clean and transform his data, estimate OLS regressions of sugar demand on log-transformed sugar prices, gur prices, and GDP per capita, and diagnose his results using Augmented Dickey-Fuller tests for stationarity, variance inflation factors for multicollinearity, and Durbin-Watson statistics for autocorrelation. He then refined his model by first-differencing the series, to account for autoregression, and included with a lagged sugar price variable. From his demand estimates, he constructed a chain of accounting identities — using average sugar recovery rates, molasses recovery rates, and ethanol conversion ratios — to derive predictions for India's annual ethanol production, which he validated against actual output data through graphical analysis.

Michael's project required graduate level statistics training. He plainly recognized his own limitations rather than overstate his findings. His willingness to report his understanding of the truth in light of these limitations is commendable. Few students follow that path.


Michael  expresses a desire to grow through service by becoming more disciplined, improving under pressure, and learning how to lead and support others. Based on what I observed, he already possesses a strong foundation in each of these areas. Military service will sharpen those qualities further, and I believe the Army will find in Michael a recruit who is trainable, dependable, and motivated by genuine commitment rather than convenience. I recommend him without reservation.

Sincerely,

James Caton
